Method: Three-Layer Bayesian Model

1 Discrete material — Dirichlet–Categorical (closed-form conjugate update)

Predictive posterior (probability that a new observation belongs to material i given observed set Z) f(z = i | Z, α) = ᾱᵢ / Σⱼ ᾱⱼ ,   ᾱᵢ ← αᵢ(0) + Σ_{m: cₘ=i} λ·pₘ

λ = evidence strength per observation; pₘ = VLM confidence. Confidence weighting means a hesitant VLM contributes less; the confidence distribution across material candidates is itself a signal of semantic ambiguity.

2 Continuous property — confidence-weighted moments (streaming, no history storage)

Accumulators + posterior mean/variance Wᵢ = Σ pₘ,   Sᵢ = Σ pₘψₘ,   Qᵢ = Σ pₘψₘ²  →  μᵢ = Sᵢ/Wᵢ,   σᵢ² = max(Qᵢ/Wᵢ − μᵢ², ε)
Final prediction = Gaussian mixture (one mode per material, weights from Dirichlet posterior) f(ψ | Z, α) = Σᵢ (ᾱᵢ / Σⱼ ᾱⱼ) · N(μᵢ, σᵢ²)

3 Uncertainty — Normal–Inverse–Gamma (NIG) prior

Total predictive variance decomposition (key contribution) Var[ψᵢ] = E[σᵢ²] + Var[μᵢ] = β̃/(ᾱ−1) + E[σᵢ²]/κ̃
aleatoric: within-class observation noise  ·  epistemic: insufficient/conflicting evidence, shrinks as confident evidence accumulates

NIG closed-form updates: κ̃=κ+pₘ, τ̃=(κτ+pₘψₘ)/(κ+pₘ), ᾱ=α+pₘ/2, β̃=β+pₘκ(ψₘ−τ)²/(2(κ+pₘ)) — all online increments, suitable for streaming reconstruction.

Outdoor Generalization (RUGD / RELLIS-3D, qualitative)

Single RGB produces material segmentation, friction, Young's modulus and total uncertainty. Cluttered scenes (dry leaves vs wood, mud vs grass) where SAM fails to separate → uncertainty rises sharply; large uniform surfaces (gravel, grass) → low uncertainty and stable predictions. Uncertainty is sensitive to segmentation quality and honestly reflects evidence reliability, which is the evidence for "calibrated".

Limitations and Engineering Notes

Stated by the paper: ① Sensitive to SAM segmentation quality — when masks merge visually similar materials, property estimates inherit the ambiguity; future direction is VLM-guided segmentation refinement; ② Shadow evolution not modeled; ③ Material parameters are set manually, no automatic assignment.
Engineering view (our additions): ① Code not released — "Coming Soon" at the CVPR 2026 paper stage, reproduction waits for the official release; ② GPT-5 is a closed commercial API, each inference costs money, performance with open VLM (Qwen-VL, InternVL) is unknown, no ablation; ③ SAM→GPT-5 two-stage error cascade — Bayesian confidence weighting mitigates but cannot remove it; ④ "Per-point" is really "per-material" — properties are estimated per material class then propagated to points; a wrong class corrupts all its points; ⑤ Small evaluation sets (6 friction points, 15 objects), outdoor only qualitative.

Implications for the Robot Project

Output maps directly to PMAT__muXXX: per-point friction → region aggregation → PMAT__mu080/mu100 material names → feed the IsaacLab friction parsing chain, an order of magnitude faster than manual labeling. This is one of the core routes in our physical-property-estimation review.
Uncertainty = active sampling guidance: high-epistemic regions are exactly where we should physically measure friction. More engineering-friendly than single-point estimates, it tells us "where it is worth another measurement".
Complements GaussGym: GaussGym solves "where the scene comes from" (scan to sim in minutes), PhysGS solves "how to label scene physics" (per-point friction/hardness). Together they close GaussGym's self-admitted gap of uniform asset physics parameters.
Adoption path: wait for official code → or follow the EVORA / Ewen et al. self-built route with an open VLM + simplified Bayesian fusion (the VLM+RAG prototype is the fastest first step, see the physical-property-estimation page).
